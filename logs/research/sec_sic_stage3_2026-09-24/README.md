# Stage 3 — bounded SEC SIC form and identity validation

**Decision:** SEC SIC scales to ten deliberately chosen issuers with complete periodic observations in ten two-year windows. Both broader-form transition timing and dated public identity add useful evidence. This supports one further gated research stage, **not** universe-wide historical classification or production use. SEC SIC is not historical GICS.

**Scope and frozen baseline.** Read the committed SIC pilot, second-stage report/results/code, sector-readiness audit and corrected historical-baseline/provenance report first. Reused their evidence; did not rerun their analyses. The accepted baseline is the September 24 02:51:28 UTC snapshot: 14,742 membership-qualified events and 191 exact signals. Stage 3 saves only compact event dates/keys and identity/catalogue records from those artifacts. No live database access, prices or return calculations were needed. Selection used identity complexity, transition evidence and evidence sparsity, never returns or signal success.

**Retrieval:** 120 attempts; 117 successful HTTP 200 responses (102 headers, 15 primary documents); three connection failures, including two sandbox failures. All failures count even when arrival at SEC cannot be established. No HTTP denial/rate limit occurred. No successful URL was fetched twice. 65 prior header responses for these issuers were hash-verified and reused; 23 of the 80 periodic window observations were already available. Total loaded issuer observations including outside-window seed/later evidence: 167; observations inside the windows: 125. Ledger reservation is persisted before each request, concurrent fetches are locked, redirects are disabled, and the cap spans resumptions. No more requests are authorized within this stage.

**Selection and observation density.** Exactly ten unique CIKs and eleven stable securities; Alphabet supplies two classes. Date windows are inclusive January 1 through exclusive January 1 two years later. Available counts are all forms in the frozen local/public SEC submissions catalogue, not counts of SIC-eligible issuer filings. Completeness means all eight periodic accessions in each such catalogue are header-verified; it does not establish completeness of every SEC form or vintage. No periodic amendment/transition report appears in these windows, although Rule A recognizes 10-K/A, 10-Q/A, 10-KT and 10-QT families.

**Does SIC require a historical ticker?** The evidence supports a conditional answer: once the correct stable security-to-CIK relationship at the cutoff has been established independently, issuer SIC retrieval/lookup needs CIK and accession, not ticker. Alphabet’s separate classes share issuer SIC; FB/META retains the same CIK/SIC across an explicitly announced ticker change; former issuers remain retrievable with empty current ticker arrays. Therefore a ticker display gap alone does not alter issuer SIC under a sound binding. It is not safe to generalize that all missing ticker history is harmless: it can reveal an incorrect issuer join, wrong share class, ticker reuse, or price-series discontinuity. Unresolved CIK binding, parent/child confusion and cross-CIK reorganizations affect SIC correctness. Missing display labels after independent identity verification primarily affect provenance/display; price-symbol continuity remains separately material to returns. Keep local IDs separate and use UNKNOWN for validated security classification when the issuer binding is unresolved.

**Former issuers and timestamp finding.** ATVI and TWTR each have eight verified periodic observations plus three broader observations in their two-year window. All these SIC codes are stable (7372 and 7370 respectively). Their historical primary documents still expose ticker/common-stock identity. Both have zero local events/filings in the frozen baseline: missing local data demonstrably does not mean missing SEC evidence. No missing events were manufactured, and these windows do not establish later merger/delisting boundaries.

The final header/catalogue cross-check found **22 acceptance-time disagreements**, exactly all 11 sampled window headers for each former issuer. All forms and accessions match. Thirteen header UTC instants are four hours later than their catalogue ISO-Z timestamps; nine are five hours later. Example: ATVI `0001628280-21-002828` header is 2021-02-23 16:19:41 Eastern = 21:19:41Z, while saved public submissions says `2021-02-23T16:19:41.000Z`. The catalogue repeats the header wall clock with a Z suffix. This is retained as a source disagreement, not repaired or treated as authority to reinterpret every ISO timestamp. The other 103 window observations agree with their catalogues. All SIC lookups and transition bounds here use verified header acceptance, never the disputed catalogues. This reinforces the need to quarantine clock conflicts before larger-scale use; no production timestamp parser or stored value changed.

**Recommended policies and next bounded scale.** Periodic filings alone are sufficient for the 69 measured event lookups, but insufficient for the earliest verified usable transition evidence and some between-event coverage. Keep the complete periodic family as a mandatory baseline. Next test a separately reviewed issuer-FILER 8-K/8-K/A layer; both known transitions gain useful timing evidence from it. Retain verified SD/proxy/11-K/13F observations as form-tagged supplemental research evidence, with explicit issuer-role checks and form-specific review before giving them equal default lookup authority. Do **not** automatically adopt unrestricted Rule B from this purposive sample. Absence of conflicts in 45 selected additional headers is not a measured assurance of safety across all forms. Exclude owner/subject-only metadata; conflicting latest codes remain AMBIGUOUS; gaps never imply proven continuity.

Recommend an evidence-backed security-to-CIK relationship ledger, separate ticker/class records, acceptance timestamps and asserted effective dates stored separately, source hashes, and review status per relationship. Public headers verify issuer identity; cover-page class context prevents equity/debt or class confusion; explicit corporate-action announcements support boundaries. Review the local-ID bridge rather than inventing interval starts. Do not merge GE/GEHC or GOOG/GOOGL. A missing ticker alone is not an automatic SIC UNKNOWN when a dated issuer binding is independently sound; unresolved issuer binding is.

Next, **only if separately authorized**, use a 20-issuer research roster (these ten reused plus ten new deliberately adverse identity cases), approximately two years each, capped at **240 new SEC attempts**. Prioritize reviewed security/CIK bindings, the former-issuer timestamp discrepancy, prewindow seed observations, and complete periodic plus issuer 8-K coverage where the cap permits. Include cross-CIK reorganizations/ticker reuse rather than another easy-only sample; disclose unfinished coverage if the cap binds. This is a planning recommendation, not permission to run it. Universe-wide classification remains blocked by unreviewed effective-dated bindings, sampled broader-form coverage, source-clock discrepancies and unchanged baseline provenance limitations. Point-in-time membership, signal, train/test boundaries and the 30-calendar-day horizon remain unchanged.
